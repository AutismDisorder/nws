# nws Commercial License Terms

> **This document is a template.** It describes the commercial licensing
> model for nws. It is not legal advice; have a lawyer review it before you
> take anyone's money.

nws is published under the GNU Affero General Public License v3
(`AGPL-3.0-or-later`, see `LICENSE`). AGPL is free for everyone, but it has
copyleft obligations: if you embed nws in software that you distribute or
expose over a network, your combined work must be released under AGPL too.

A **commercial license** removes those obligations. The code is identical —
one codebase — you are buying *terms*, not a different artifact.

## What a commercial license grants

- Use, modify, and embed nws in closed-source products and services,
  including public and internal network services, without any AGPL
  obligation on your own code.
- Use of all present and future versions released within the license term.
- Support from the maintainers (email; priority tier for Enterprise).
- Permission to state that your product is "powered by nws".

## What it does not grant

- Ownership of the nws code (the maintainers keep the copyright).
- Any warranty. The software is provided "as is", like the AGPL version.
- Indemnification against third-party IP claims (can be added for
  Enterprise under a negotiated contract).

## Tiers

| Tier       | Price (per year) | Scope                                             |
|------------|------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| Developer  | $99              | One named developer, any number of projects       |
| Team       | $499             | Up to 5 developers at one company                 |
| Enterprise | $2,500 – $10,000 | Unlimited developers at one legal entity/site, priority support |

Why these numbers work for buyers: renting article extraction from an API
vendor costs $/request and adds up fast at RAG-scale volumes. Self-hosting
nws behind these terms is cheaper, runs on their hardware (no data leaves
their network — a real security/compliance win), and a one-time annual fee
beats metered billing. At $10,000/year the Enterprise tier is deliberately
under-priced against the API-alternative math.
